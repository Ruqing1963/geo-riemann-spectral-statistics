# geo-riemann-spectral-statistics

**Statistical comparison of Phanerozoic geological boundary spacings with Riemann zeta zero spectra using random matrix theory (GOE/GUE).**

## Key Finding

Does Earth's geological heartbeat follow random matrix statistics?

We test 538 million years of Phanerozoic stage boundaries against GUE, GOE, and Poisson distributions — and find **level repulsion at β ≈ 1** (GOE universality class).

| Test | Poisson | GOE | GUE |
|------|---------|-----|-----|
| KS *D* | 0.2177 | **0.0896** | 0.1329 |
| KS *p* | 0.0001 | **0.3824** | 0.0551 |
| Anderson–Darling *A²* | 6.794 | **1.176** | 5.461 |

- **Poisson rejected** at *p* = 0.0001 and 11.6σ in spacing ratio
- **GOE best fit** across all test statistics
- **GUE marginal** (*p* = 0.055), consistent with ⟨*r*⟩ but rejected by higher-order tests
- **Control validated**: Riemann zeta zeros confirm GUE (*D* = 0.050)

> Earth's tectonic events are not random — they exhibit correlated timing with weak level repulsion, like eigenvalues of a GOE random matrix.

## Methodology

### Data Sources

- **Geological boundaries**: 102 stage-level boundaries from the [International Chronostratigraphic Chart (ICS 2023/09)](https://stratigraphy.org/chart), spanning 538.8 Ma (Fortunian) to 0.0042 Ma (Meghalayan). After filtering sub-Holocene microintervals (ΔT < 0.1 Ma), 99 valid intervals remain.

- **Riemann zeta zeros**: First 500 non-trivial zeros computed via `mpmath.zetazero()` at 15-digit precision. Spacings normalized using the asymptotic density ρ(γ) = (2π)⁻¹ log(γ/2π).

### Theoretical Models

| Model | PDF | Level Repulsion | Physical Analog |
|-------|-----|-----------------|-----------------|
| Poisson | *e*⁻ˢ | None (β = 0) | Independent random events |
| GOE | (π/2) *s* exp(−π*s*²/4) | Linear (β = 1) | Time-reversal symmetric systems |
| GUE | (32/π²) *s*² exp(−4*s*²/π) | Quadratic (β = 2) | Broken time-reversal symmetry; Riemann zeros |

### Statistical Tests

1. **Kolmogorov–Smirnov test**: Maximum CDF deviation
2. **Anderson–Darling statistic**: Tail-sensitive goodness-of-fit
3. **Nearest-neighbor spacing ratio ⟨*r*⟩**: Normalization-independent diagnostic
4. **Higher moments**: Variance, skewness, kurtosis
5. **Bootstrap confidence intervals**: 10,000 resamples for ⟨*r*⟩ and Var(*s*)

## Physical Interpretation

The GOE-class level repulsion in geological boundary spacings implies that **major tectonic events are not independent**. After a significant boundary event, the Earth system enters a refractory period during which stress must re-accumulate and ecosystems must recover before another major transition can occur. This creates the characteristic suppression of small spacings seen in random matrix ensembles.

The classification at β ≈ 1 (GOE) rather than β = 2 (GUE) suggests that while geological correlations are strong enough to decisively reject Poisson randomness, they do not reach the intensity of the repulsion seen in the Riemann zeta spectrum.
